🧭 The End-to-End Pipeline in 5 Steps

1. Synthetic Data Stream

Streamed 50,000 stories from roneneldan/TinyStories into a local corpus (~44 MB) with explicit <|endoftext|> separators.

2. Custom Byte-Level BPE Tokenizer

Trained a 4,096-vocab tokenizer from 256 byte primitives with zero out-of-vocabulary (<unk>) failures.

3. Modern Decoder Architecture

Implemented RMSNorm, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), SwiGLU FFN, and FlashAttention-backed Causal Self-Attention.

4. FP16 Mixed-Precision Training

Optimized with AdamW, Cosine Annealing, linear warmup, and weight-tying regularization.

5. Interactive Sampling CLI

Enabled text generation with Temperature, Top-K, and Nucleus (Top-P) probability filtering.

🔤 What is Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE)?

Computers cannot read English letters. They only calculate numbers. Tokenization turns text into numbers.

Strategy How It Works Why It Fails
Word-Level Every unique word gets a number. ❌ Huge 500k dictionary; crashes on typos, slang, or new names (<unk>).
Character-Level Every letter gets a number. ❌ A 100-word story turns into 600 numbers. The model spends all its capacity learning to spell.
Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) Starts with bytes, repeatedly merges most frequent pairs. ✅ Frequent words stay whole ("the"); unknown words break into syllables. Never crashes!

⚖️ The "Vocab Size Trap" in Small Models

Why didn't we just use the standard ChatGPT / GPT-2 tokenizer with 50,257 tokens? Here is the exact parameter math:

The Embedding Formula

Embedding Table Parameters = Vocabulary Size × Hidden Dimension (d)

Tokenizer Choice Embedding Table Cost ($192 \times V$) Transformer Brain Parameters Model Resource Balance
GPT-2 Vocab (50,257 tokens) 9,649,344 (~9.65M) ~1.77M ❌ 85% of model is just a dictionary lookup!
Our Custom Vocab (4,096 tokens) 786,432 (~0.78M) ~1.77M ✅ 70% of model is dedicated to Transformer reasoning!

Weight Tying Innovation

At the end of the Transformer, the LM Head projects the hidden state back to vocab size ($192 \rightarrow 4096$). By weight-tying the output projection matrix to the input embedding matrix ($W_{\text{out}} = W_{\text{embed}}^T$), we saved another 786,432 parameters (~23% of total model) and prevented overfitting!

🧠 Micro-LLaMA vs. Legacy GPT-2

We avoided the 2019 GPT-2 design and implemented the state-of-the-art LLaMA 3 / Mistral (2024–2026) architecture:

Feature Legacy GPT-2 (2019) Our Micro-LLaMA (2024–2026) Why Modern Wins
Normalization LayerNorm (Centers & scales) RMSNorm Removes mean-centering; saves ~7% compute with identical mathematical stability.
Positional Embeddings Absolute Learned Positional Embeddings RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings) Rotates Q and K in complex 2D planes; encodes relative distance naturally and generalizes past training length.
Feed-Forward Standard GELU MLP ($xW_1 \rightarrow \text{GELU} \rightarrow W_2$) SwiGLU Gated Activation Gated multiplication with SiLU yields significantly faster convergence and lower cross-entropy loss.
Attention Engine Explicit $Q \cdot K^T / \sqrt{d}$ matrix multiplication FlashAttention / PyTorch SDPA Fuses softmax and causal masking into C++/CUDA kernels for high token throughput. 
# The Micro-LLaMA Decoder Block in PyTorch (src/model/llama.py)
class TransformerBlock(nn.Module):
    def forward(self, x, freqs_cis):
        # 1. Pre-RMSNorm + Rotary Causal Self-Attention
        h = x + self.attention(self.attention_norm(x), freqs_cis)
        # 2. Pre-RMSNorm + SwiGLU Feed-Forward
        out = h + self.feed_forward(self.ffn_norm(h))
        return out

⚡ Training & Generation Pipeline

1. Next-Token Prediction & Initial Loss Math

A language model is an auto-complete predictor. It outputs probabilities for all 4,096 tokens in our dictionary.

At Step 1, before learning anything, each token has equal random probability ($1 / 4096$). Therefore, the theoretical initial loss is:

Initial Loss = -ln(1 / 4096) = ln(4096) ≈ 8.318

In our actual smoke test log, our initial loss was 8.358! This mathematically verified our clean random weight initialization.

2. Hardware Optimization for RTX 3050 (4GB VRAM)

  • FP16 Mixed Precision (AMP): Halves tensor memory from 4 bytes (FP32) to 2 bytes (FP16).
  • AdamW Optimizer with Decoupled Decay: Weight decay (0.05) is applied strictly to 2D matrices, ignoring 1D biases and RMSNorm gains.
  • Cosine Annealing with Warmup: Ramps learning rate smoothly to avoid gradient shock in early iterations.
  • Gradient Clipping (1.0): Prevents destabilizing gradients during training.

3. Autoregressive Sampling Controls

In src/generate.py, we control how the model outputs text:

  • Temperature: Controls probability sharpness (0 = greedy/repetitive, 0.8 = balanced story creativity).
  • Top-K: Restricts sampling to the top 40 candidates.
  • Top-P (Nucleus): Restricts sampling to the dynamic cumulative 90% probability mass.

🎤 Team Presentation Defense Guide

Anticipated questions your team or tech lead might ask during your presentation:

Q1: Why did you train on TinyStories instead of Wikipedia or Common Crawl?

Answer: High-entropy open-web datasets require billions of parameters to avoid generating incoherent gibberish. The Microsoft Research TinyStories paper demonstrated that domain-bounded language with simple syntax allows ultra-compact models under 10M parameters to master coherent grammar, story progression, and reasoning.

Q2: Why not just fine-tune an existing model using LoRA / QLoRA?

Answer: The goal was to build an SLM from foundational first principles. Writing raw PyTorch tensors, implementing RoPE complex rotations, creating the custom BPE tokenizer, and writing the training loop gives complete ownership of every component and zero external black-box dependencies.

Q3: Why did you use RoPE instead of ALiBi?

Answer: RoPE is the universal standard in modern state-of-the-art models (Llama 3, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen). While ALiBi uses a static attention bias, RoPE rotates tokens in 2D planes, preserving fine-grained syntactic precision while still maintaining relative distance decay.

Q4: Why did we build our own tokenizer instead of using the HuggingFace LLaMA-3 tokenizer?

Answer: LLaMA 3 has a 128,000 token vocabulary. For an SLM with hidden dimension 192, a 128k tokenizer would require an embedding matrix of $128,000 \times 192 \approx 24.5\text{ Million parameters}$—making the model almost 10× larger solely due to the dictionary! Our 4,096 BPE keeps the model compact at 2.56M total parameters.
